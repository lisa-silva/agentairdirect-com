The real problem

The company's reputation wasn't weak. Its digital record was.

MD Spangler Concrete had decades of real-world experience, licensing, completed work, and business history. But the information that explained all of that was scattered across names, records, platforms, and time.

A person could eventually piece it together. An AI system should not have to guess.

The job was simple to say—and hard to do: make the business make sense.

What had to become obvious

Who is this?

MD Spangler Concrete, the contractor identity, and the historical company record.

What do they actually do?

Foundations, driveways, walkways, structural, commercial, decorative work, slabs, and flatwork.

Where do they work?

Redding plus the business's approved Northern California service-area context.

How does a customer act?

Phone, email, address, hours, and a direct estimate path.

What we did

We didn't “optimize a page.” We built the record AI needed to understand the business.

Then we tested it hard enough to find where our own work still fell short.

01 · RESOLVE THE IDENTITY

Stop making machines guess which business is which.

The site distinguishes MD Spangler Concrete as the current customer-facing business while preserving Spangler Concrete & Engineering, Inc. as relevant historical professional context. Two records. One explainable professional history.

02 · BUILD THE FIRST-PARTY SOURCE

Give the business a place it controls.

We published a dedicated MD Spangler Concrete website using authentic project photography and clear information about identity, services, contact details, service area, qualifications, and customer action.

03 · MAKE THE FACTS MACHINE-READABLE

Visible for people. Explicit for machines.

The implementation included relevant structured data, service knowledge, metadata, image alternative text, contact information, local context, and identity details aligned with what visitors can actually see.

04 · AUDIT OUR OWN WORK

This is where it got interesting: we got a C.

The first corrected measurable baseline was 52 of 81 eligible points: 64/100, grade C. We could have stopped because the site looked good. We didn't. The score showed us exactly where the foundation still needed work.

05 · FIX IT. RE-RUN IT. VERIFY IT.

Same denominator. Better implementation. 100/A.

After correcting the website and cleaning up the audit methodology, the final production run reached 81/81 eligible points, normalized to 100/100, with 15 passes, 0 warnings, and 0 failures.

Then we tested the real question

Could an outside AI actually understand what we built?

What Perplexity understood

It got the important pieces.

  • MD Spangler Concrete as the current business identity
  • A clearly identified business owner
  • The relationship to the earlier company identity
  • Contractor-license information
  • Specific concrete services
  • Geographic relevance and service area
  • Direct customer contact and estimate information

Perplexity described the page as an understandable local-business entity and said it could potentially serve as a factual source. Useful evidence? Yes. A universal citation guarantee? No.

Then Perplexity got something wrong

That taught us just as much.

It surfaced an older version of the page with placeholder wording that had already been removed from production.

We checked the live site instead of blindly changing it again. The obsolete text wasn't there.

Lesson: fixing the source and getting every outside AI system to refresh the source are two different jobs.

The question is no longer just: “Can AI find my business?”
It's: “Which version of my business does AI currently believe?”

Where our job ends

We can fix the source. We can't pretend to be the owner.

Some changes require the business owner's credentials, authorization, identity verification, or direct action with the platform that owns the record.

That doesn't make the foundation unfinished. It defines the next phase.

What this proves

A claim is cheap. A measured before-and-after process is harder to fake.

So here is exactly what this case study demonstrates—and exactly what it doesn't.

What we demonstrated

  • Fragmented business information can be organized into a clearer first-party record.
  • Current and historical identities can be connected without pretending distinct legal records are the same company.
  • Visible content and machine-readable information can be aligned.
  • The implementation can be audited, corrected, and re-measured under one methodology.
  • An independent AI retrieval system was able to extract the core business facts during testing.

What we do not promise

  • Search rankings
  • AI citations
  • Recommendations
  • Customer inquiries
  • Contracts or revenue
  • Instant refresh by every outside system
Why Agent Air Direct exists

Your business is not invisible to AI. Your information may just be impossible to connect.

Businesses already leave signals everywhere: websites, licenses, directories, reviews, social profiles, service pages, old names, new names, locations, phone numbers, and customer actions.

When those signals disagree or live in disconnected places, AI doesn't get a clean story. It gets fragments.

Agent Air Direct helps turn those fragments into a clearer, owner-directed, machine-readable business foundation—and then monitors the gap between what the business has published and what outside systems actually believe.

Visibility is the beginning. Trusted representation is the future.
